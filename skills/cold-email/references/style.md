# Cold email style (from real emails that got founders and CTOs to reply)

## Who
- Startups (< 50 people): a founder, the CTO or a founding engineer. They read their own email.
- Bigger companies: the hiring manager or team lead, not HR.
- Funding news, a new product launch or a hiring post = a good reason to write now.

## The first line
Your first line decides if they read the second one.
- One real, specific thing about *them*: their product, a post, a launch, a line in the job ad that stood out.
- Light and true. Never a fake compliment, never "I hope this finds you well".
- It must come from a page you actually read. Keep that URL in the log.

## Show, don't introduce
> I prefer to let what I build speak louder than a traditional self-introduction:

then **one** sentence on **one** project that fits their product, plus a link (demo, GitHub, 1-minute Loom).

## Template (under 120 words)

```
Subject: <Role> at <Company>: built something for you

Hi <first name>,

<one human line about them or their product>

I came across your <role> opening and wanted to reach out directly.
I prefer to let what I build speak louder than a traditional self-introduction:
<one sentence on the project> → <link>

<one line: why this matches their stack/product>

Would you be open to a 15-minute chat this week?

Best,
<name>
<LinkedIn> · <GitHub/portfolio>
```

## One clear ask
A 15-minute call, or "who should I talk to about this?". Never "any opportunities you may have".

## Follow-up (once)
After 5 days with no reply: 2–3 sentences, something new (an update, a demo), the same single ask. Then move on.
Ghosting is the job market, not you.

## Checklist (the linter checks the first four)
- [ ] Under 120 words
- [ ] No generic opener / no placeholders
- [ ] One project + one link
- [ ] One clear ask (a question)
- [ ] Right person (founder / CTO / hiring manager), name only if a source shows it
- [ ] First line is about them and is sourced
